# Benchmark: company-specific interview data (/company-questions)

Date of run: 2026-10-21. Repo: /Users/akshat/Desktop/groundwork. No repo file edited. Measurement scripts are in the scratchpad (measure.ts, m2.ts, m3.ts, m4.ts, m5.ts, m6.ts, urls.tsv).

Legend: OBSERVED = I ran a command or read the page. INFERRED = my conclusion.

## Summary verdict

Groundwork is not top notch on the raw company data. It is top notch on curation, honesty and explanation, and that is a real, uncommon advantage. It is not better than the big players on volume, recency or coverage of recent reports, and it cannot be, because it reads public pages by hand and the richest sources block it.

- Better than raw reports: yes, for model answers. Each answer is a written solution with numbers, trade-offs, follow-ups and mistakes. Raw reports on Blind, LeetCode, Glassdoor and Naukri give a question title and a few lines. Nobody else (Hello Interview, Aced/Exponent, GfG) pairs a company-labelled prompt with a labelled evidence grade and a runnable solution at this price point (free).
- Not better than the big players on data: 135 answers and 22 process pages against, for one company alone, 455 Flipkart experiences on Jointaro and 351 Swiggy search results on Blind (both OBSERVED, below). Groundwork has 357 unique source URLs for everything combined. Its newest-report coverage is thin: 46 of 193 answer-source links carry no year, and only 66 of 135 answers have a source from 2025 or later.
- Verifiable: yes. 59 of 60 randomly sampled source URLs returned 200 (the one failure was LeetCode, a bot block). Five of six claims I traced to the cited page checked out in full. One did not (see test 6).
- Honest: best in class among what I read. 22 process pages each carry a "gaps" field that says which pages were blocked and what is therefore guide-only. No competitor I read does this.
- Biggest weakness: the labels say "reported", but the underlying evidence is one to three public pages per answer, and for the Indian start-ups it is mostly GfG posts from 2020-2021 and prep-guide pages. The process pages lean on the competitors' own guides (Hello Interview, Aced, TechPrep) for the 2026 shape of Google and Meta. That is fine as long as the owner does not claim independent data.

## Scope and method

Section: /company-questions on https://groundwork.austincoders.com (HTTP 200 OBSERVED; page text read via fetch). Code: content/system-design/company-questions (types.ts, process-_.ts, rounds.ts, people-_.ts, cs-*.ts, batch1-8.ts, dsa-batch1-11.ts, lld-batch1-5.ts) and app/company-questions.

Commands run (all OBSERVED):

- `npx tsx scratchpad/measure.ts` counts questions, evidence grades, source kinds, years, hosts, process rounds.
- `npx tsx scratchpad/m2.ts` (CS and SQL sets, answer word counts), `m3.ts <company>` (dump a process page), `m4.ts` (URL list, level buckets, grade-versus-source audit), `m6.ts`.
- A link check: 60 random source URLs out of 357 unique, `curl -L` with a normal user agent, 15 s timeout.
- Fetched and read competitor and official pages (URLs cited inline). Blocked pages are named and not guessed.

Competitors read: Hello Interview, Aced (formerly Exponent, redirect OBSERVED from tryexponent.com), Jointaro, Blind, interviewing.io, Levels.fyi, Atlassian, Meta, Amazon and Google official pages, plus LeetCode, Glassdoor, Naukri Code 360, AmbitionBox, GfG via search results only (those pages returned 403/404 to fetch).

### Six tests, Groundwork versus the public record

1. Meta (global). Groundwork says optional OA, recruiter call, 45-minute phone screen, roughly four-interview loop, AI-assisted coding round as a pilot, team matching. Hello Interview's E4 guide (https://www.hellointerview.com/guides/meta/e4) gives the same stages and the same 4 to 8 weeks. Meta's own 2022 careers post (https://www.metacareers.com/blog/preparing-for-your-software-engineering-interview-at-meta/) confirms the 45-minute screen split (5 intro, 35 coding, 5 questions) and the coding, design, behavioural trio. Verdict: no mismatch. Groundwork adds the contested bootcamp point and says the guides disagree; HI E4 says team matching replaced bootcamp in 2023. Honest.
2. Google (global). Groundwork: GHA, one phone screen, 2 to 4 coding rounds, system design from L5, hiring committee and team matching, order contested, code-comprehension pilot. Hello Interview Google L4 (https://www.hellointerview.com/guides/google/l4) says the same on every point, including GHA about 50 items, 30 to 45 minutes, and no dedicated L4 design round. Groundwork's official-source gap statement ("careers pages loaded without readable content") reproduced for me: both google.com careers URLs returned navigation only. No mismatch, but the page is built from guides, not primary data.
3. Atlassian (global). The official page (https://www.atlassian.com/company/careers/resources/interviewing/engineering) lists coding (data structures and code design), a 60-minute systems design, manager interview, values interview, debrief and hiring committee. Groundwork matches all five and correctly says the official page gives a duration only for system design. Mismatch: none. Groundwork adds a recruiter screen and a Karat-run screen from a guide only, and labels them guide/one. Honest.
4. Amazon (global). The official page (https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interviewing-at-amazon) lists online application, assessments, phone screening, interview loop, no durations, no loop size. Groundwork's rounds do not contradict it and its gaps field says the official page is thin. interviewing.io's Amazon guide (https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/amazon) says 4 to 6 interviews in the loop with a Bar Raiser and does not mention India. Groundwork's SDE-2 evidence is one candidate report (roundz.substack.com), so the SDE-2 loop is under-sourced. Mismatch: none; thin on level.
5. Swiggy (India). Groundwork lists a "machine coding" round as guide-only and writes that none of the candidate reports it could read include it. Public LeetCode Discuss posts surfaced by search describe a 90-minute machine-coding or LLD round on an inventory system for Instamart (https://leetcode.com/discuss/post/8314817/swiggy-sde-2-by-anonymous_user-izkb/) and a GfG SDE-2 write-up lists a machine coding round (https://geeksforgeeks.org/swiggy-interview-experience-set-3-sde-2). A July 2025 SDE-2 offer post (https://leetcode.com/discuss/post/7073156/) describes three rounds with no machine coding. So the round exists in some loops. This is an under-grading, not a false claim, and Groundwork's gap note says LeetCode returned 403. Its SDE-3 report details matched the source exactly (https://roundz.substack.com/p/interview-experience-123-swiggy-sde3-l8: July 8 2025, L8, JSON parser, promotional notification design). Mismatch: Swiggy machine coding grade "guide" should be at least "one". Also Swiggy's 2025 SDE-2 volume on LeetCode (at least six posts found by one search) is not in the sources.
6. Razorpay (India) and a data-integrity check. Groundwork says almost all of Razorpay is guide-based and the only candidate report is an undated campus intern page. Search shows LeetCode posts for SSE (2024, https://leetcode.com/discuss/interview-experience/5817444/), Lead SE (https://leetcode.com/discuss/interview-experience/7360702) and a Jan 2026 Glassdoor report, all describing machine coding, HLD, hiring manager, HR. The shape matches Groundwork's. The evidence base is thinner than it needs to be.

Claim-to-source trace (6 answers, all OBSERVED by fetching the cited page):

- Uber Alien Dictionary, grade "several": Jointaro SDE II Jan 2024 page names Alien Dictionary, CodeSignal screen then one algorithm round. Confirmed. The other two sources are company-tagged lists, so "several" is one candidate plus lists.
- Swiggy SDE-3 rounds: confirmed in full.
- Flipkart ticket booking (BookMyShow): confirmed in the GfG page updated Apr 2024 (rounds began about March 2024).
- Zomato max-sum path: confirmed in GfG page updated Jan 2021. That is 5.8 years old. Still graded "several" with 2020 and 2021 sources.
- Microsoft distributed rate limiter, grade "several": the Blind Office 365 thread has a one-line Feb 2020 comment naming a distributed API rate limiter. The second cited Blind page (microsoft-sde2-interview-prep) did not show any rate-limiter mention in what I could read (comments may be hidden from the fetch). So "several" rests on one verified 2020 line and one unconfirmed. Treat as one.
- Self-audit of grade rules (m4.ts): the site's definition of "several" is two or more independent pages with at least one candidate write-up. All 31 "several" answers have at least two sources and one candidate report; 6 of 31 have both sources on one host (two different posts of the same site), which is defensible but not proof of independence. No "one" answer lacks a candidate report. No "guide" answer claims a candidate report.

Link check (OBSERVED, 60 random of 357 URLs): 59 returned 200, one 403 (leetcode.com, bot block). Link rot looks low; source quality cannot be inferred from this alone.

### Depth sample

- Coding: Uber Alien Dictionary answer has clarifying questions, brute force, insight, edge cases, 4 follow-ups, common slips, complexity, a dry-run trace and 13 test cases. LeetCode, Jointaro and Blind give the title and, at best, a line.

- Behavioural: Amazon "unclear requirements" gives a frame, why-it-is-asked and sources, and links to the interview book. Comparable to Aced/Hello Interview behavioural guides, free.

## Where Groundwork is ahead

1. Honesty system. Three grades with definitions ("two or more independent public pages, at least one a candidate write-up"), shown on each card, plus per-company gap notes (e.g. Zerodha: no first-hand engineering report could be read; Ola: pages last updated 2021 or 2022). OBSERVED in page text and process-*.ts.
2. Answers, not just reports. Model answers with numbers, trade-offs, follow-ups and slips. Uber Alien Dictionary: trace plus 13 tests. OBSERVED.
3. Same product holds process, prompts, behavioural, CS and SQL, and chapters. A candidate can go from "Swiggy SDE-2 loop" to a runnable LLD exercise without leaving the site. OBSERVED (EXERCISE_OF has 66 links, LLD about 14 checks).
4. Conflicts are stated, not smoothed: the Google GHA length, the Meta bootcamp, the committee and team-match order. OBSERVED in Google and Meta rounds.
5. No paywall. Aced shows answer counts and sign-in/pricing links (OBSERVED), LeetCode company tags are premium (see docs/competitor-audit-2026-10.md L6).

## Where it is behind

1. Scale and recency: described above. Groundwork's candidate-report evidence is concentrated in GfG (69 links in answers, 79 in rounds), Roundz and a few blogs.
2. It does not read the richest sources. Its own gap fields say LeetCode, Glassdoor, Naukri Code360, Medium and Blind often returned 403/429 (Swiggy, Zomato, Groww, Paytm, Ola, Juspay, Dream11, Razorpay and Uber notes). So 2025-26 SDE-2 and SDE-3 reports, the most useful ones, are exactly what is missing. Three of six traced tests found this gap. OBSERVED.
3. Process pages depend on competitors' prep guides. Google and Meta 2026 shape rests on Hello Interview, Aced and TechPrep pages; no official interview-length data for Google, Meta, Amazon or Microsoft was readable. For Meta, an official page exists but dates from 2022. INFERRED: if Hello Interview changes, Groundwork drifts with it.
4. No level lens. Level strings are free text: 90+ variants such as "SDE-2 (L61)" or "level not stated" (26 answers have no level). Users cannot filter SDE-1, SDE-2, senior or staff.
5. No freshness metadata. No "last verified", no per-source access date, 54 answer sources with no year.
6. No contribution or correction loop. No way to submit "I was asked this".
7. Companies missing: Adobe, Oracle, Salesforce, Walmart, Goldman and other finance, Intuit, Stripe and AI labs, TCS/Infosys/Wipro/Cognizant/Accenture, Zepto, Rippling and similar. Aced lists several of these.
8. Thin per-company depth for some Indian start-ups: Zerodha 6 round sources, Dream11 5, Meesho 8 (OBSERVED). Dream11 timeline comes from one report.

## Hard truths

1. The data is anecdotal, small and mostly secondhand. 135 answers rest on 1 to 4 public pages each; 81 answers have exactly one source. The site says so, but a reader skimming the "widely reported" badge (35 items) may read it as stronger than it is. Of the 31 "several" answers, 18 have fewer than two candidate-report sources (the rest of their support is prep guides or lists), and 6 have both sources on one host. The definition permits this; a reader may not realise.
2. The honest "several" claims can drift. Microsoft rate limiter is the proof: a 2020 one-liner plus an unconfirmed page. I checked six; one failed, which is a sample, not a rate.
3. It cannot out-collect the leaders. Jointaro lists 455 Flipkart experiences; Blind shows 351 Swiggy results; Aced shows 5,402 questions. Groundwork reads each source by hand and the most useful (LeetCode Discuss, Glassdoor, Naukri) block it. A solo author cannot catch up on volume. The only credible position is "smaller, cleaner, honest, with solutions".
4. Process pages for Google and Meta mostly restate Hello Interview and Aced. That is legitimate synthesis and the site discloses the gaps, but the owner should not describe it as independent research.
5. Recency is weak: 25 dated answer links are 2022 or older, 54 have no year, and 70 percent of behavioural-question sources have no year. Indian start-up processes change fast (Zomato is now Eternal, and its 2020-21 GfG reports predate that).
6. No community, no submissions, no counter of "asked this" votes. The data is static from the day it is written.
7. The page carries no site-wide update date (OBSERVED in page text), while it says reports "may be out of date".
8. Claims on the site that the repo supports: 135 answers, 22 companies, 93 CS/SQL questions, 35 "widely reported". The "80 diagrams" figure in the page text I did not verify (I did not count diagrams).
9. Most of the scores for LeetCode, Glassdoor, AmbitionBox and Naukri are inferred from search snippets and prior knowledge, not read from the pages.
