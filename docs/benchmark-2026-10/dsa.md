Date of reading: 2026-10-21. Repo: /Users/akshat/Desktop/groundwork (no file edited). Live site spot-checked: https://groundwork.austincoders.com/problems/ex-trapping-rain-water

Legend: OBSERVED = I read or ran it. INFERRED = my conclusion from observed facts. Competitor pages were read in a normal browser or fetch, public pages only, nothing behind a login or paywall was opened. Gated content is reported as gated, not guessed.

## Summary verdict

Groundwork's DSA course is genuinely top tier in explanation depth and in per-problem teaching design, and it is the weakest of the group on scale, trust signals, language reach and on tests that enforce what the briefs promise. It is not yet "better than the big companies' data" as a whole. It is better than the free tiers of NeetCode, GeeksforGeeks and LeetCode Explore on written teaching of patterns. It is behind on problem volume (694 against NeetCode's 1,126 and LeetCode's 3,800+), on languages (JavaScript-first, Java cannot be run), on hidden-test judging, and on any outside credibility (no sources, no community, one author).

Numbers that matter (all OBSERVED, commands below):

- 48 written DSA chapters, 396,884 words in total (about 8,270 each, about 6,450 of prose once code is removed). 694 DSA exercises, 818 quiz questions. 955 is the site-wide exercise count and includes 99 JS and 162 React, so "955 runnable interview problems" on /problems overstates DSA by 261.
- All 694 reference solutions pass their own tests; no starter passes. That part is solid.
- The 22 false claims and bugs the 19 Oct audit found are still in the source. I re-confirmed five of them. Fix work has not started.
- Only 73 of the 281 exercise briefs that state an O(...) target have a large input that would catch a slow solution (heuristic, see section on practice). An O(n squared) Trapping Rain Water passes all 8 tests.
- NeetCode 150 coverage: 143 of 150 matched (7 absent).

Where the owner should be most careful: the claim "better than the big players" is supportable for chapter depth on a spread of patterns, and not supportable for practice volume, judging, languages, trust or community.

## Head-to-head samples

Nine samples. For each: what the competitor page showed, what Groundwork has, and a verdict. No text is quoted.

### 1. Trapping Rain Water (two pointers, hard) against NeetCode

- NeetCode (OBSERVED, https://neetcode.io/problems/trapping-rain-water/question and /solution): statement, constraints, topic tags, "recommended time and space" box, four hints, company tags, acceptance rate, discussion (105 posts), a video, a written solution with at least brute force, prefix and suffix arrays and a stack, an intuition and algorithm list for each, complexity, nine languages. Step-by-step visualisation per approach, but "Upgrade to Pro to unlock visualizations" is displayed.
- Groundwork (OBSERVED, `content/dsa/dsa-two-pointers.ts`, section "Trapping rain water: three ways to the same answer"): three versions (arrays, two pointers, monotonic stack), a prose proof of why the lower side is settled, a hand-traced dry-run table of the classic input (I checked the final total of 6 and the meeting at index 7), a "say it like this" interview script. The exercise `ex-trapping-rain-water`: 8 tests, 3 hints, solution code only.
- Differences: Groundwork has the proof and the interview script; NeetCode has the brute-force-first ladder, video, discussion, acceptance data and nine languages. Groundwork's exercise cannot tell a slow solution from a fast one: an O(n squared) version passes 8 of 8 tests (OBSERVED, run in node), while the hint promises O(1) space and O(n) time.
- Verdict: Groundwork ahead on explanation, behind on judging and ecosystem.

### 2. Sliding window against GeeksforGeeks

- GfG (OBSERVED, https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/dsa/window-sliding-technique/, marked last updated 1 Apr 2026): about 8,300 characters of page text including navigation, one fixed-window worked example (max sum of k elements), naive then expected approach with code in six languages, a short description of fixed and variable windows, a short "how to identify" list with constraint hints (n up to 10^6, O(n) or O(n log n)), then links to problem lists. No variable-window worked example on this page, no pitfalls section, no proofs. The page also carries course and job promotions.
- Groundwork (OBSERVED, `dsa-sliding-window.ts`, about 5,600 words): fixed, variable, a template, counting windows, at-most-K distinct, "exactly K is at most K minus at most K-1", minimum window substring with need and missing counters, anagram windows, budget windows, a section on when the window breaks (negative numbers), sliding window maximum with a deque, pitfalls, recognition cues, a step visualiser, 16 exercises.
- Verdict: clearly ahead.

### 3. Binary search on the answer (Koko Eating Bananas) against NeetCode

- NeetCode (OBSERVED, https://neetcode.io/problems/eating-bananas/solution): about 5,000 characters, two approaches (linear scan, binary search), a "common pitfalls" block (arbitrary upper bound, integer overflow in total time), visualisations Pro-locked.
- Groundwork (OBSERVED, `dsa-binary-search.ts`, about 5,500 words): the loop-from-invariant derivation, boundary templates, "the feasible(x) template: minimize the maximum", real-valued search, duplicate-finding by value range, median of two sorted arrays, a range-collapse visualiser, pitfalls, 25 exercises including `ex-koko-eating-bananas` and `ex-ship-packages-in-days`.
- Verdict: ahead on teaching the family. NeetCode's pitfall list is a good template Groundwork already matches in spirit.

### 4. Grid DFS and BFS (Number of Islands) against NeetCode

- NeetCode (OBSERVED, https://neetcode.io/problems/count-number-of-islands/solution): about 9,400 characters, prerequisites listed with links to its beginner lessons, three approaches (DFS, BFS, union-find), complexity, visualisation locked. The recursive DFS is given with no note on recursion depth.
- Groundwork (OBSERVED): `dsa-grid-bfs.ts` (about 11,100 words including code) covers direction arrays, BFS layers, visited-on-enqueue, parent pointers, a queue without O(n) shift, multi-source BFS, flood fill with a measured recursion limit, eight directions, reverse from the borders, state-space BFS, bidirectional BFS. The chapter states the recursion limit with a measured figure (about 10,373 frames on one engine).
- Defect (OBSERVED, run in node 22): the reference solution for `ex-number-of-islands` is recursive, and a solid 90 by 90 grid already throws RangeError. The real problem allows 300 by 300 and the exercise's largest test is 40 by 40. The grid-bfs chapter warns about this; the exercise and the matrix chapter do not. The audit reported the same defect (passes at 90, fails at 95).
- Verdict: chapter ahead; exercise reference solution below the real problem's constraints.

### 5. Topological sort against cp-algorithms

- Verdict: ahead on interview patterns, behind on correctness polish; cp-algorithms is terse but tighter.

### 6. Union-find against cp-algorithms

- Verdict: ahead on breadth of interview variants; on par on core theory; cp-algorithms has Arpa's trick and range painting which I did not find in Groundwork (grep "arpa" returns nothing).

## Where Groundwork is ahead

1. Written pattern teaching. On the six samples with a readable competitor page (sliding window, trapping rain water, binary search on the answer, number of islands, intervals, tries) Groundwork's version is deeper: it derives the idea, has dry-run tables I could verify, lists pitfalls, and ends each chapter with a recognition guide and interview wording. NeetCode's free pages are shorter and are built around code plus a video; GfG's page is a stub by comparison.
2. Teaching what the pattern family looks like. Chapters group 10 to 17 variants of a pattern (for example "exactly K is at most K minus at most K-1", "state-space BFS", "offline union-find"). Competitors teach one problem at a time.
3. Reviewable correctness of the practice set. 694 of 694 reference solutions pass, no starter passes, and the exercise runner and tests are the same code the site ships. That is a better mechanical record than most sites publish.
4. Free access to what others gate. NeetCode puts visualisations and most of its courses behind Pro (observed "Upgrade to Pro" text and a gated lesson 8). Groundwork's chapters, quiz pools and exercises are open.
5. In-browser multi-language running. The language menu lists runs-here: JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, SQL, C++, C, Ruby, PHP, Lua. The student can solve in Python or C++ with no server.
6. Step debugger on the learner's own code on every exercise (Debug button; trace and debug panel in `PracticeWorkspace.tsx`).
7. Quiz depth. 818 DSA questions with a reason per choice. I saw nothing comparable in the NeetCode or LeetCode pages I opened (LeetCode's forum post advertises 12 quizzes in its crash course, which I did not open).

## Where it is behind

1. Problem volume and judging. 694 against NeetCode All (1,126, 824 free), LeetCode (3,800+), takeUforward (495 sheet, so Groundwork is above the sheet only). No hidden tests, no acceptance rate, no submissions count, no company frequency per problem on the DSA exercises. Tests are all visible in a Tests tab (OBSERVED in the workspace code), which is fine for learning and weak for judging.
2. Tests do not enforce what briefs say. Heuristic (regex on test bodies for generated inputs of 1,000 or more): 120 of 694 exercises have such a test; 281 briefs mention an O(...) target; 73 of those 281 have a large input. Observed: O(n squared) Trapping Rain Water passes all tests. The audit lists 21 more.
3. Languages. Java, C#, Go, Rust, Kotlin and Swift are "write only" in the workbench (cannot run). Chapter code is JavaScript only except two chapters (binary search, two pointers) with Python, Java and C++ translations for 4 marked blocks (`content/dsa/code/*`, `grep -o 'data-code="'`). NeetCode shows nine languages on every solution.
4. Solutions are bare code. 691 of 694 solutions have no comment at all and there is no explanation field in the Exercise type (`content/types.ts`), so "reveal solution" shows code with no approach, no complexity and no alternatives. NeetCode shows intuition, algorithm, complexity and several approaches per problem.
5. Visualisation volume. 7 step demos in 7 of 48 chapters. NeetCode has a step player per approach (locked, but it exists). Groundwork has 106 static SVG figures.
6. Known errors still in the source. The audit of 19 Oct says nothing was changed; I confirmed five items still in source or behaviour: `powMod(5n, 0n, 1n)` returns 1n (should be 0n); recursive `numIslands` reference overflows at 90 by 90; the union-find alpha(2^65536) = 5 sentence; "return order is exactly reverse post-order"; the "Right-Node-Left reversed" label (the procedure described is right, the label is wrong).
7. Chapter length against the site's own standard. `docs/content-standard.md` sets 3,500 to 5,000 prose words and 6,000 as a hard cap. Measured (strip code blocks, count words): 35 of 48 chapters exceed 5,000 and 25 exceed 6,000; the longest, games-probability-dp, has 13,145 prose words. The chapters are best-in-class reference material, but many are not something a student reads in one sitting (about 8,300 words with code is roughly 40 minutes).
8. Coverage tail: flow and matching, Johnson, suffix tree and automaton, advanced number theory, HLD and centroid, DP optimisations, balanced BSTs. cp-algorithms covers all of these.
9. No video. NeetCode and takeUforward are video-first for most learners (NeetCode problem pages show "Video Explanation"; takeUforward's PLUS tier lists videos).
10. Metadata: 24 of 694 exercises are in no chapter's practice list (`unl.out`), so they are reachable only from /problems.

## Hard truths

1. The scale gap is permanent for a solo project. 694 problems against NeetCode All's 1,126 and LeetCode's 3,800 and counting: Groundwork cannot close the volume gap and should not try. The honest position is "fewer problems, each taught better", and that is only true once gaps 2, 3 and 6 are done.
2. The strongest claim needs the cleanup first. The headline "every claim reviewed" and "audit 8.8 accuracy" are the owner's own audit; the audit itself says 22 false claims, 110 misleading statements and exercises that promise more than their tests check, and none of it is fixed. Any public statement about accuracy above the big sites is premature until those are fixed and re-verified.
3. Solo-author risk. The chapters list one author ("Akshat") and an update date of August 2026 in the meta; there are no references in any of the 48 chapters, no named reviewers, and the independent reviews were agents. Readers deciding to trust a 396,000-word course will look for outside validation.
4. No community and no real candidate data. There is no backend (the only API routes are client-error, joke and tts), progress is stored in the browser, there are no discussions, no ratings, no acceptance rates, and "asked at" data on company pages is reported, not measured. LeetCode's problem page shows hundreds of discussion posts and millions of submissions; none of that exists here and cannot be generated by writing.
5. Reading load. A learner who does one chapter a day for 48 days is looking at about 40 minutes of reading each, before exercises. The competitor model, especially takeUforward and NeetCode, is video plus short text. Groundwork needs a short path, not just a long reference.
6. Exercise tests are public and small. 6 to 23 tests each (average 8.3), all visible, many with tiny inputs. A student can pass with hard-coded or slow solutions and the site will report success. That matters more than another 100 problems.
7. The site's own number is easy to misread. /problems and the metadata say 955 problems; 261 of those are JS fundamentals and React components. For a DSA buyer the right number is 694.
8. The audit's NeetCode 150 count (143) is the same as mine, but the claim should read "143 of 150 NeetCode 150 problems have an exercise or close equivalent, 7 do not" until item 4 is done.
9. Chapter claims about engines. The audit flags V8-specific facts (array growth, `shift()` thresholds, recursion limits, `Math.max(...array)` limits) stated without engine versions; those are engine-specific and drift. I did not re-measure them.

## What I could not verify

- NeetCode: video contents, hidden-test quality, how many of the 1,126 problems have written solutions, the full 20 and 35 lesson texts (gated), whether its code is correct (I read, did not run). The company tags and acceptance rates beyond one problem page.
- LeetCode: the Interview Crash Course chapter list (card page did not render), editorials (the tab exists, I did not open it), premium gating, total problem count (I only saw the highest id, 3863).
- GeeksforGeeks: only one article read in full; site-wide accuracy cannot be judged from one page. No problem counts are shown on the pages I read.
- takeUforward: module names and the content of the sheet (page rendered only the header numbers); video quality; free versus PLUS split beyond the label.
- AlgoMonster: course size, pattern count, what is free; the marketing page and the flowchart gave no counts, so every AlgoMonster cell in the scorecard is "not observed".
- cp-algorithms: the structure descriptions come from a summarizing fetch tool; I did not read the articles end to end, so claims such as "no Kahn's algorithm" and "no rollback DSU" are the summary's, not mine.
- Groundwork: I did not re-run the audit's reviewer fuzzing; I re-ran the mechanical pass (694 of 694 solutions pass, no starter passes) and re-checked five audit findings. Timing numbers (the slowest solutions) are from one laptop under vitest and will differ in a browser. The "large test" counts come from a regex heuristic on test bodies and may miss tests built another way or count loops that are not performance tests. Prose word counts strip `<pre>` blocks only; tables inside prose are counted, so the figures slightly overstate prose compared with the site's own rule that excludes tables of code. I did not time page loads or test on phone widths. Whether the workbench's Python and C++ runs actually grade correctly for every problem was not tested end to end (I confirmed the menu and read the grading code).
- Legal: Groundwork's exercise briefs are rewordings of well-known problems; I made no assessment of how close they are to the originals.
